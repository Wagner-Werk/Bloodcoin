1. Summary

Bloodcoin is an open-source project that records the weekly number of deaths in the United States, as published by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), on a public ledger. The same figure adjusts how many new coins are issued each week, within fixed limits.

The project has two technical parts. The first is the Bloodcoin chain, a small proof-of-work blockchain with its own explorer and a demo exchange that uses test money. The second is BCUS, a token contract on the Base network. BCUS currently exists only on the Base Sepolia test network, where it has no value.

No coins or tokens are sold. There is no pool, no pre-sale and no fundraising.

3. Purpose

Most cryptocurrencies issue new coins on a fixed schedule that ignores the world around them. Bloodcoin ties its issuance to one of the most carefully recorded public statistics there is: weekly deaths in the United States. Every week the count is written into the ledger, where it stays permanently and can be checked by anyone.

The project is also an art chronicle. Each report is a trace of the dead that the chain keeps. The project treats this data as a public record, with respect. It does not collect personal data about any deceased person; it uses only national weekly totals published by the CDC.

BCUS is also implemented as a token contract (ERC-20) on Base, an Ethereum layer-2 network. It consists of four contracts. All parameters are fixed when the contracts are deployed and cannot be changed afterwards.

6. The mortality oracle

6.1 Data source

The oracle uses the weekly death counts for the United States published by the CDC on data.cdc.gov. Recent weeks are revised for some time after publication, so only weeks that are at least eight weeks old are used.

6.2 Calculation

  1. Recent deaths: the total of the four most recent usable weeks.
  2. Baseline: the average total of the same four calendar weeks in the three previous years.
  3. Deviation: if recent deaths differ from the baseline by three percent or less, the target factor is 1.0. Beyond that, each further percent of excess mortality lowers the target by five percent, and each percent below the baseline raises it by five percent. The target always stays between 0.5 and 1.5.
  4. Step limit: the actual factor moves towards the target by at most 0.05 per report. A single report, even a wrong one, can therefore change issuance by at most five percent.
  5. Plausibility checks: the contract rejects reports outside 80,000 to 1,000,000 deaths and reports where recent deaths and baseline differ by more than a factor of two.

6.3 Signed reports and trust model

Each Monday a script on the publisher's server reads the CDC data, calculates the figures, signs them with the oracle key (EIP-712) and submits them to the oracle contract. The signing key is held by the publisher and is used for nothing else.

Honest limitation. Whoever holds the oracle key could submit plausible but false figures. The contract only catches gross errors. The protection is transparency: every report, together with the raw CDC data it was calculated from, is published on the oracle page, and anyone can check it against data.cdc.gov directly in the browser. The oracle can be retired permanently with a signed message, after which it accepts no further reports.

There is no pre-sale, no private sale, no team allocation beyond the founder allocation and no marketing reserve. A mortality factor below 1.0 means fewer tokens are created in that week; the difference is not created later.

8. Staking

All newly emitted BCUS go to the staking contract, which distributes each week's emission evenly over seven days among the addresses that have deposited BCUS, in proportion to their deposit. Deposits can be withdrawn at any time.

The amount anyone receives depends on the emission, the mortality factor and how much others have deposited. It is not fixed, not guaranteed and not a return promised by the publisher. Figures shown on any staking page are estimates of the mechanism only. Whether staking affects the regulatory classification of BCUS is part of the legal review (section 12).

9. The demo exchange

The exchange at exchange.bloodcoin-us.com is a demonstration. Users trade coins of the Bloodcoin chain against dollar balances that are test money with no value. Dollars cannot be paid in or out.

  • Coins deposited from the Bloodcoin chain are credited after 20 confirmations and can be withdrawn at any time, for a fee of 0.001 BCUS.
  • Withdrawals are on-chain transactions and cannot be reversed.
  • The exchange publishes its reserves so that anyone can check that customer balances are covered.
  • The exchange does not trade BCUS tokens on Base and does not handle real money.

11. Risks

  • Total loss. Any token can lose its entire value or never have one.
  • Software errors. The contracts and the chain may contain errors that lead to loss of tokens. No independent audit has been completed.
  • Oracle risk. The oracle depends on one key and on CDC data. Wrong, delayed or missing data, or misuse of the key, affects issuance within the stated limits.
  • Centralisation. The Bloodcoin chain runs on very few nodes. It can be stopped, rewritten or attacked by whoever controls the majority of mining power.
  • Concentration. The founder allocation is large compared with early emission. If staked, it would receive a large share of new tokens.
  • Liquidity. There is no market. There may never be one.
  • Regulation. Laws and their interpretation can change and may restrict or prohibit the project, its use or its transfer.
  • Key loss. Lost private keys cannot be recovered by anyone.
  • Reputation and access. Because the project uses mortality data, banks, exchanges, app stores and platforms may refuse to work with it.
  • Tax. Holding or receiving tokens can have tax consequences. Users are responsible for their own taxes.
  • Project risk. The project is run by one person. It can be delayed, changed or discontinued.

12. Regulatory status

The publisher is resident in Switzerland. Under the practice of the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA), tokens are classified by function as payment, utility or asset tokens. BCUS grants no claim against anyone, which points towards a payment token; whether staking changes that assessment is open.

  • A Swiss law firm has been asked to review the classification and the obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA). The review is not complete.
  • No BCUS will be sold, and no trading pool will be opened by the publisher, unless the review permits it and any required affiliation with a self-regulatory organisation is in place.
  • Bloodcoin is not offered to persons in the United States and not offered to the public in the European Union.
  • This document is not a crypto-asset whitepaper under the EU Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), because no offer to the public in the EU is made.
